# Channel Economics

For products sold through multiple channels, do not treat all revenue as economically equivalent.

Distinguish End User, Channel Customer/Retailer/Distributor, Economic Buyer, and Marketplace/Payment Intermediary.

Per-channel map:
`End price → recognized revenue → COGS/direct cost → gross margin → channel-specific variable costs → contribution before shared overhead`

Possible channel costs: wholesale discount, outbound freight, marketplace commission, payment processing, returns, fulfillment, sales commission, performance marketing, support and store operations.

Rules:
- DTC often has higher gross margin than wholesale, but not automatically higher contribution.
- Wholesale may trade unit margin for reach, lower direct CAC and lower fulfillment burden.
- Marketplace adds discovery/demand but also fees and dependency.
- Studio/salon/gym/retail can be both distribution channel and purchase gatekeeper for small products.
- If channel contribution is not disclosed, mark it `INFERENCE` or `UNKNOWN`.
