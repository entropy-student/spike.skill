## Why does this exist?

A normal trending list answers: **“What is hot today?”**

Entertainment Rander asks something more useful:

> **“What is the Chinese internet actually playing with—and which memes are becoming shared entertainment language or full meme universes?”**

A celebrity story can dominate hot search without becoming a meme. A phrase can be repeated everywhere without generating meaningful remix culture. A dance can have billions of views while everyone copies the exact same template. Meanwhile, an old variety show can suddenly become S-tier when users keep inventing new interpretations, characters, edits, and theories around it.

The skill therefore measures **creative afterlife**, not raw attention.

---

## Remember the whole model in four lines

> **B — People say it.**  
> **A — People imitate it.**  
> **S — People keep building on it.**  
> **S+ — People use it to create the next meme.**

The key boundary between A and S is:

> **Are people copying—or creating?**

---

## The four tiers

| Tier | Essence | Typical participation |
|---|---|---|
| **S+** | Meme grammar / cultural asset | Used to explain and create later memes |
| **S** | Generative meme universe | People extend, reinterpret, excavate, and remix it |
| **A** | Mass imitation | People make their own version of a stable template |
| **B** | Catchphrase / repetition | People mainly say it |

### Calibration examples

- **S+**: Zhenxue (甄学), Mingxue (明学, boundary case)
- **S**: *Divas Hit the Road 2* archaeology, *The First Half of My Life* archaeology, Mailin / “I’m not recording anymore”, Skill Gomoku, “Huai Min was still awake”, “OMG you scared me”, “一天一天贴近你的心”
- **A**: Subject Three dance, Good Night Miss, Dig Dig Dig, Dinosaur Kanglang, “青天大老爷” when longevity is still unproven
- **B**: “你真是饿了”, Shuan Q, Tai Ku La, Zun Du Jia Du

## Eight evaluation dimensions

1. **Entertainment value** — does it feel fun and participatory?
2. **Cross-circle reach** — does it escape the original fandom, show, or platform?
3. **Longevity** — days, weeks, months, or recurring revivals?
4. **Remixability** — rewrites, edits, dubbing, parody, reenactment, archaeology.
5. **Source independence** — can it survive outside the original source?
6. **Derivative potential** — can it spawn child memes, characters, vocabularies, “schools,” or universes?
7. **Creative density** — are users copying one template or inventing genuinely new layers?
8. **Internet-native feel** — overly mechanical or tacky formats are capped; contextual, absurd, or high-density formats score higher.

Two hard gates:

- **Entertainment value must pass.** A huge but serious cultural topic does not automatically belong in an entertainment meme radar.
- **S-tier requires creative expansion.** Repetition or identical imitation is not enough.

---

## Common misclassifications

- **Everyone knows it ≠ S-tier.** Awareness is not remix culture.
- **Huge view count ≠ S-tier.** Billions of views can still describe an A-tier copy template.
- **A long-lived phrase ≠ S-tier.** If people mainly say it, it stays B; flexible adaptation may push it to A.
- **No catchphrase is required.** A whole show or character ecosystem can be S-tier through sustained archaeology and reinterpretation.

## Quick start

```text
Use the Entertainment Rander standard to identify Chinese internet entertainment memes that actually matter.
Do not treat celebrity news, hot-search ranking, or raw view counts as meme power.
Evaluate entertainment value, cross-circle reach, longevity, remixability, source independence, derivative potential, creative density, and internet-native feel.
Rank candidates as S+ / S / A / B: B = people say it; A = people imitate it; S = people keep building on it; S+ = it has become reusable meme grammar.
Prioritize content archaeology, character schools, absurd meme systems, and sticky formats that keep generating new creative layers.
```

---

## Research protocol

When web research is available, do not rely on a single hot-search list. Look for:

1. cross-platform spread,
2. original derivatives rather than reposts,
3. persistence or multiple revival waves,
4. participation by different groups,
5. new uses outside the original source.

One article, one ranking, or one large number is supporting evidence—not the verdict.
