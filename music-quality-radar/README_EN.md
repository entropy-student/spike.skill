## What problem does it solve?

Most listeners can say only two things:

> “I like it.”
>
> “I don't.”

The harder questions are:

- Why does it sound good?
- Is the melody strong, or is the arrangement doing the work?
- Why does the chorus feel satisfying?
- Why can a simple lyric feel powerful while another feels generic?
- Why do some polished AI songs still feel obviously AI-generated?
- If I made this song, what should I fix first?

**Music Quality Radar** turns those questions into a simple evaluation system.

---

## Seven dimensions

| Dimension | The simple question |
|---|---|
| **Melody** | Is there a line you remember after the song ends? |
| **Arrangement** | Does the song know when to add, remove, build, and release? |
| **Lyrics** | Does it show a real image or only state generic emotions? |
| **Emotional arc** | Does the feeling develop, or remain flat throughout? |
| **Performance** | Does it feel expressed, or merely performed correctly? |
| **Production** | Is the sound clear, balanced, and comfortable? |
| **Identity** | After a few seconds, does the song feel recognizably itself? |

---

## Rating system

> **B — Competent but ordinary**  
> **A — Good and well-made**  
> **S — Excellent and memorable**  
> **S+ — Benchmark-level; the song feels uniquely itself**

Complexity is not quality. Popularity is not quality. Expensive production is not quality.

The core question is:

> **Do melody, arrangement, lyrics, performance, sound, and emotion work together toward the same goal?**

---

## Quick start

Give an AI a song, audio file, lyrics, or an AI music prompt and say:

```text
Evaluate this using Music Quality Radar.
Use plain language rather than heavy music terminology.
Explain why it works or does not work, assess melody, arrangement,
lyrics and emotional progression, identify the strongest point,
the biggest weakness, the best improvement, and give an S+ / S / A / B rating.
```

## Current status: v0.1

This is a **calibration build**.

The intended workflow is to test it against real songs you love and songs you find ordinary, then refine the boundaries through examples and counterexamples.
